## Changes made

Downloaded the full-resolution Figure 1 from the Springer Nature static
image endpoint (2121 × 1510 PNG), then:

1. Cropped to panel **e** alone (region 748 × 630 at offset +1356 +864).
   The remaining panels — a, b, c and d — are not reproduced.
2. Painted a white rectangle over the residual `e` panel letter in the
   top-left corner, which the crop clipped rather than removed.
3. Added a 16 px white margin and resized to 900 px wide.

No data, label, branch length or date in the panel was altered.

## Why this panel

It is the picture of the claim the Science page makes in prose: that
*H. elevatus* is a hybrid lineage, that roughly 0.7% of its genome came
from *H. melpomene* and 99.3% from *H. pardalinus*, and that the split
happened around 180,000 years ago. The two labelled arrows carry the
whole argument, and the three wing photographs show that the hybrid does
not look like a blend of its parents — which is the point.

Panels a–d were left out deliberately: a is a broader phylogeny, b a
range map, c a haplotype network and d a set of topology-weighting
trees. None of them are readable at web size, and none are needed for
the sentence this figure supports.
